Short, narrated, animated explainers on military history, history and philosophy. One idea, one battle or one thinker per Reel. Secular: no faith content (that lives on Pensive Minds).

## Format (single Reels, not carousels)

- 30 to 50 seconds, 1080x1920, 30 fps, narrated, captions burned in (most people watch muted).
- **Structure:** hook in the first 2 seconds (a date, a number, a stake) → three or four beats → a hard payoff number or line → cliffhanger or follow prompt.
- **Visual language:** charcoal background with film grain and vignette; brass (#C49242) for highlights; bone (#E9E4D8) for friendly forces and text; red (#B23428) for the enemy; sea #16262C. DM Serif Display for big text and captions, DM Sans Bold for labels. Animated top-down maps with unit blocks, arrows, distance brackets, camera push-ins and impact shakes; big counters for casualty or army figures. Gemini stills or clips may be used for scene-setting.
- **Header on every frame:** "CAMPAIGN No. N" (brass) / "TOPIC · DATE" (muted) / "RANKS & REASON" (top right). Exception: the header may fade out behind the dim on payoff frames (the big casualty counter and the closing number / Part 2 card), so the payoff stands alone. It stays visible on every map and story beat.
- **Captions:** two lines max per beat, one brass highlight word, inside the safe zone (y ≈ 1250 to 1450).
- **Sound:** narration first; synthesized drum score underneath (tom hits on beats, accelerating drums into a charge, a boom on the decisive moment), ducked under the voice. Target about -16 dB mean, peaks under -1 dB.

## Accuracy rules

- Every number, quote and claim traces to a named source (primary where possible: Herodotus, Thucydides, Caesar, Clausewitz, Sun Tzu in a named translation, etc.), with modern scholarship noted where the ancient figure is doubted ("per Herodotus").
- Keep a Sources log: Reel, claim, source.
- Separate legend from record (example: the Marathon-to-Athens run is a later legend; Herodotus records the run to Sparta).
- No invented quotes. Paraphrase unless the translation is public domain.

## Monetization (standing rule, Chris, Oct 13, 2026)

Every Story, post and Reel is built to earn, not just to post. The page makes money in this order, so content serves whichever stage is current:

1. **Now (no follower minimum): own the audience.** Grow followers and an email list. Lead magnet: a free "Campaign Dossier" per Campaign (map, timeline, key numbers, sources) behind an email signup in the link in bio. The list is the asset that survives algorithm changes.
2. **Affiliate: ON HOLD (Chris, Oct 13, 2026).** No affiliate links until following and reach are built. Later: link only the books we actually cite, labeled as affiliate links.
3. **When eligible: Instagram's own tools.** Gifts on Reels, Reels revenue share where available, then Subscriptions (extended cuts, early access, subscriber-only Campaign polls). Eligibility needs a professional account, age 18+, a supported country and compliance with Meta's Partner and Content Monetization Policies; follower minimums vary by feature and country, so check the Monetization section in the Instagram app rather than trusting third-party lists.
4. **With an audience: sponsorships.** Brand-safe partners that fit (history books and audiobooks, documentaries, strategy and history games, map prints). Always use Instagram's "Paid partnership" label.
5. **Later: own products.** Paid dossier packs, prints of Campaign maps, a paid newsletter tier.

**Every piece of content:**
- **Hook non-followers first.** Most reach comes from people who don't follow yet, so frame 1 / slide 1 must work with no context: a number, a date, a stake.
- **Earn the send.** Sends per reach is reported as Instagram's strongest signal for reaching new people. End with a specific share prompt tied to the topic ("Send this to the friend who…"), not a generic "share this".
- **Earn the save on carousels:** dense, reference-worthy facts (numbers, maps, timelines) and a "Save for later" line.
- **Ask one real question** in the caption (who was right, what would you have done). No "comment YES" bait; Meta demotes engagement bait.
- **Build return viewers:** numbered Campaigns, Part 2s, and a teaser for what's next on every post.
- **Searchable first line:** the caption opens with the plain search terms ("Battle of Marathon, 490 BC") before the story. 3–5 specific hashtags.
- **Point to the ladder:** on posts tied to a Campaign, mention the free dossier ("Free map and timeline: link in bio"). Stories use the link sticker once a destination exists.
- **Stay advertiser-safe:** factual, no gore, no glorifying violence, no current wars or living politicians (same as Guardrails); this also keeps Reels eligible for monetization.
- **Stay original:** all visuals made by us, no watermarked or ripped clips, no compilations. Originality is part of Meta's monetization review.
- **Measure what pays:** Recon reports sends per reach, saves, follows per post, profile visits and link clicks, not just likes.

**Current priority (Chris, Oct 13, 2026): followers and reach first.** Every post optimizes for sends, saves and follows; no selling yet. **Open items (need Chris):** pick an email service for the dossier signup (Beehiiv or Kit both have free tiers) and set the link in bio. Once those exist, Campaign No. 1's dossier is the first lead magnet.

## Guardrails

- Educational, not glorifying: no gore, no graphic imagery, no celebration of killing.
- HOLD anything about current wars, living politicians or ongoing conflicts.
- No real living people named in a promotional way; no brands.
- The narrator is an AI voice and never claims to have served or to have been there.

## Pillars

1. **Battles that turned on one decision** (Marathon, Cannae, Agincourt, Midway).
2. **Commanders and their choices** (historical figures only).
3. **Ideas that shaped war and life** (Sun Tzu, Clausewitz, the Stoics, Machiavelli).
4. **Myth vs record** (what really happened vs the legend).

## Cadence

Start at 3 Reels a week at fixed times (adjust after the first month of data). Part 2s post within 48 hours of Part 1.
